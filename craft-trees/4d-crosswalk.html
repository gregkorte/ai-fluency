Crafts and the 4Ds

Our 63 crafts compared with the AI Fluency Framework that Anthropic teaches in Claude Academy: four competencies (Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence), each with three sub-skills, plus the Description–Discernment loop and three modes of working with AI.

Planning view for Greg and John, 2026-10-30. The definitions are quoted from Claude Academy. Which crafts sit under which sub-skill is our proposal, and nothing here changes the craft tree. Click any craft for its description.

Three modes, three books

The framework names three ways of working with AI. They line up roughly with our three books. The match isn't one to one, but it's a useful way to tell John and learners where the course is heading.

Book 1: AI Fluency

Augmentation

You and AI collaborate as creative thinking and task execution partners.

Chat, prompting, the profile and checking the output. The S5 Skill build is a first step toward automation, and the read-only inbox in S4 is still augmentation, because the learner sends nothing.

Book 2: AI Workflows

Automation

AI executes specific tasks based on your instructions.

Connectors, MCP, Skills and Zapier. The tool starts writing and sending, so reviewing an action before it happens (Sign-off) becomes the core check.

Book 3: AI Orchestration

Agency

You guide AI to work independently on your behalf, shaping its knowledge and behavior rather than specific actions.

Briefs, checkpoints, drift and stop conditions. Anthropic's separate human-agent teams course (beta) belongs here, and we can compare it once Book 3 has sessions.

The Description–Discernment loop

Discernment works hand-in-hand with Description in a continuous feedback loop. The course runs the loop in four stages. Our crafts cover all four, and the last stage is what our capstones assess.

Each craft placed under its closest 4D family. The bar shows each book's mix. Book 1 leans on Description and Discernment, Book 2 adds Diligence through access and write actions, and Book 3 brings Delegation back for agent hand-offs.

Where we go deeper

Four areas where the tree names skills the framework covers in a line or not at all. These are the course's selling points next to a free Academy course.

The AI knows you

Profile, Context, Memory, Blind Spot, Revision, Continuity. Performance Description covers how the AI should behave, such as concise or detailed. It doesn't cover a profile you carry between tools, or checking what the tool remembers.

Grounded vs. general answers

Grounding, Comparison, Currency, Provenance. Platform Awareness is knowing what different AI systems can do. We teach how to check an answer differently depending on where it came from.

Access and write actions

Vetting, Ownership, Declaration, Sign-off, Targeting, Threshold, Breach. In the framework, Creation and Deployment Diligence are one sentence each. In the course they're most of Book 2.

Agent drift

Divergence, Pinpoint, Placement, Trigger, Verdict, Validation. Process Discernment judges how the AI approached one task. Book 3 judges a long run, step by step, and decides when to stop it.

What to raise

Each item is checked against the pushback list's principle: where does it land for good, and what comes out to make room?

  1. Transparency is the one real gap. Being honest about AI's role in our work with everyone who needs to know. No craft covers it. The smallest fix adds no session time: a question in the capstone's safety line ("Who needs to know AI helped, and did you tell them?"), plus a disclosure angle for John's "(maybe)" watermarking reading. A new craft would be a tree change and needs Greg's approval.
  2. One of our S2 prompting rules could read against Process Description. S2 says "describe the finished result, and skip spelling out every step". Anthropic says how the AI approaches a request can be as important as specifying the end goal. The two fit together, because our "ask it to ask you" rule is process description, but the S2 wording could use one clarifying line. Check it when Session 2 becomes the layout model (queue item 2).
  3. Platform Awareness is better covered than the first pass said. Model Sense and Grounding are Book 1 exercises, and the S3 Claude vs. Gemini Notebook exercise is the place to assess it. The first pass also put Model Sense under Problem Awareness, which Anthropic defines as understanding your goals and the work involved, so this page moves it.
  4. Task Delegation is thin in Book 1. Calibration is only Supporting there, and the capstone's "human part" is the main place learners say what stays with them. That's by design, because Candidacy, Viability and Redline come later. It's worth knowing if John compares the course to the Academy's.
  5. "Context limits" still isn't named anywhere. The Academy's capabilities course covers context limits. Learners hit them in the S3 research and S4 inbox sessions. One line in Model Sense or the S3 summary is enough.
  6. Names and licensing. "AI Fluency" is Anthropic's framework name and also the name of our course, our Book 1 and our Book 1 capstone craft. Pointing to the framework could help with positioning, but it shouldn't suggest an endorsement. Third-party summaries say the framework is licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (non-commercial). Confirm the terms before any framework text appears in the paid course's lessons. Quoting it here, for planning, is fine.
